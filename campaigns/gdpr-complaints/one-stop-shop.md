# GDPR One-Stop-Shop And Lead Authority

Practical guide for GDPR complaint campaigns that may become cross-border.

## What This Is

The GDPR one-stop-shop matters when the processing is cross-border and more than one supervisory authority could be involved.

It does **not** mean:
- the complainant must solve the whole jurisdiction question before filing
- only the authority in the controller's country can hear from the complainant
- the EDPB is the first complaint destination

## The Three Core Rules

### 1. File with a real DPA, not the EDPB

The EDPB is not the complaint intake body for ordinary cases.

Use:
- the DPA where you live
- the DPA where you work
- or the DPA where the alleged infringement took place

Official reference:
- [Can I lodge a complaint with the EDPB?](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/faq-frequently-asked-questions/can-i-lodge-complaint-edpb_en)
- [EDPB contact FAQ](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/more-about-edpb/contact-us/faq_en)

### 2. The lead authority depends on the controller's main establishment and decision-making structure

In cross-border processing, the `lead supervisory authority` is not chosen because the complainant prefers it.

The lead authority is tied to:
- the controller or processor's main establishment
- and where relevant decisions about the processing are actually taken

Official reference:
- [Guidelines 8/2022 on identifying a controller or processor’s lead supervisory authority](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-82022-identifying-controller-or-processors-lead_en)

### 3. Your local authority still matters even if it is not the lead authority

The authority that receives the complaint does not become irrelevant just because another authority may be lead.

In one-stop-shop cases, authorities cooperate. For campaign purposes, this matters because:
- the local authority can still be the complainant's visible contact point
- silence or non-progression can still be documented
- under-enforcement may be about the cooperation process as much as the underlying company conduct

Official reference:
- [One-Stop-Shop Leaflet](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/one-stop-shop-leaflet)

## Campaign Use

Use this layer when:
- a large platform or multinational is involved
- the case affects users in more than one Member State
- the controller's EU establishment and the complainant's country differ
- a complaint appears stalled because of lead-authority or cooperation dynamics

## Practical Questions To Ask

### Before filing

- Which DPA can I file with based on my residence, work, or the place of infringement?
- Is there a public sign that the controller's relevant EU decision-making sits in another Member State?
- Is this a one-country complaint or obviously cross-border from the start?

### After filing

- Did the authority acknowledge the complaint?
- Has it indicated whether the matter is cross-border?
- Has it identified or implied a lead authority?
- Has the case gone quiet without a clear procedural update?

### Before escalation

- Am I complaining about the company only, or also about cooperation delay?
- Can I explain the chronology cleanly without speculation?
- Do I have enough evidence to distinguish controller abuse from enforcement failure?

## What This Does Not Solve

- It does not identify the lead authority for a controller automatically.
- It does not replace national procedural law or court remedies.
- It does not mean every slow case is unlawful delay.

The value of this guide is narrower:
- avoid misfiling
- frame cross-border cases properly
- understand where lead-authority disputes may be coming from
- document under-enforcement without guessing
