# GDPR Complaint Targets

## Primary Targets

### Controllers

Use for:

- initial rights requests
- clarification asks
- evidence of refusal, delay, or evasion

Desired output:

- a real answer
- a documented refusal
- a traceable timeline
- where relevant, a usable clue about the controller's likely main EU establishment before cross-border escalation starts

### Data Protection Authorities

Use for:

- formal complaint
- follow-up on delay
- procedural accountability

Desired output:

- acknowledgement
- case reference
- visible progression
- where relevant, clarity on whether the matter is being treated as cross-border or one-stop-shop

Useful source:

- [gdpr-controller-establishment-map.md](/Users/kevinbrown/EU-Citizen-Engagement-Toolkit/data/national-authorities/gdpr-controller-establishment-map.md:1) for major-platform establishment clues

## Secondary Targets

### Public-interest privacy organisations

Use for:

- issue validation
- systemic framing
- cross-border escalation

Current seed routes already in repo:

- noyb
- Panoptykon Foundation
- Bits of Freedom
- La Quadrature du Net

### Journalists

Use for:

- systemic abuse
- repeat-pattern harms
- visible enforcement failure

Not ideal for:

- poorly evidenced individual disputes

### MEPs and committee routes

Use for:

- under-enforcement patterns
- cross-border accountability pressure
- institutional oversight pressure

Likely useful committee/policy surfaces:

- LIBE
- IMCO where data abuse overlaps consumer harm
- committee secretariats when a broad institutional route is needed

## Tertiary Targets

### Commission or Ombudsman-adjacent routes

Use only when:

- the issue has become one of oversight, process failure, or enforcement architecture
- the national or lead-authority stage is already evidenced

This is not the first route for ordinary GDPR rights disputes.
