# Who Can Move This

This is the power map.

Do not ask only "who can I email?"

Ask:

- who can directly fix it
- who can force movement
- who can widen the political or public cost of inaction

## 1. The Direct Actor

This is the company, platform, controller, or service owner.

Use this route when:

- you need the first clean documented contact
- the problem may still be fixable without escalation
- you need a record before going wider

Typical examples:

- platform support or complaint team
- controller privacy office
- trader or marketplace complaint route
- accessibility or service-owner contact

## 2. The Competent Authority

This is the national body with legal or supervisory power.

Use this route when:

- the direct actor ignored or mishandled the problem
- the law gives a regulator or authority direct competence
- you need an official complaint record

Typical examples:

- DPA
- Digital Services Coordinator
- consumer authority
- equality body
- accessibility monitoring body

## 3. The Political Pressure Layer

This is where elected representatives, committee secretariats, Commissioners, or cabinets matter.

Use this route when:

- the issue is systemic
- the problem is procedural, political, or about under-enforcement
- the official authority route exists but is not moving

Typical examples:

- MEP office
- committee coordinator
- committee secretariat
- Commissioner cabinet
- Commission spokesperson or DG press route

## 4. The Public-Interest Amplification Layer

This is where NGOs, rights groups, journalists, and networks help.

Use this route when:

- the issue is broader than one private dispute
- the evidence may support a public-interest story
- you need validation, coalition, or safer escalation

Start in:

- [Community Contacts](/Users/kevinbrown/EU-Citizen-Engagement-Toolkit/data/community-contacts/README.md)

## Simple Rule

- direct fix -> direct actor
- formal complaint -> competent authority
- systemic blockage -> political pressure
- wider public-interest pattern -> NGO or journalist layer

You may need more than one, but not all at once.
