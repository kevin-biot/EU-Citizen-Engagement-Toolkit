# Citizen Digital Needs Roadmap

This roadmap shifts the toolkit from "institution reference" toward "citizen action infrastructure".

The core design principle is simple: in a digital world, citizens do not just need contact details. They need to know where to go, what to save, how fast to act, and what to do when the first route fails.

## Priority Layers

### 1. Issue-to-Route Guidance

Citizens usually start with a problem, not an institution.

Examples:
- My platform account was suspended without explanation.
- A website is using dark patterns.
- I cannot get my personal data from a service.
- An AI system made a harmful decision about me.
- A public digital service is inaccessible.

The toolkit should answer:
- what kind of issue this is
- which route is fastest
- which route creates a formal record
- which route is national
- which route is EU-level
- what evidence should be saved first

### 2. Evidence Discipline

Digital harms disappear quickly. Citizens need a repeatable evidence routine:
- screenshots
- full URLs
- timestamps with timezone
- archived pages
- copies of emails and platform notices
- device and account context
- chronology of events

Without this, good complaints collapse on proof.

### 3. National + EU Layering

Most modern digital problems sit across more than one level.

Typical combinations:
- national data protection authority + GDPR rights route
- national consumer authority + CPC network logic
- national Digital Services Coordinator + Commission oversight context
- sector regulator + Ombudsman or access-to-documents route

The toolkit should help citizens combine these layers instead of treating Brussels as the only destination.

### 4. Time and Escalation

Citizens need help with timing:
- when to try informal contact
- when to formalise
- when to escalate
- when silence itself becomes a procedural issue

This means adding deadline and escalation logic to each playbook.

### 5. Plain-Language Action

The toolkit should be readable by:
- ordinary citizens
- SMEs without in-house counsel
- journalists
- NGOs
- people working in a second language

Every playbook should answer:
- what happened
- why it matters
- what to do in the next 24 hours
- what to do in the next 7 days
- what to do if the first route fails

## Build Order

### Immediate

- Digital issue playbooks
- National authority schema
- Issue router

### Next

- Evidence templates
- Deadline tracker
- Topic-to-officeholder routing

### Later

- Monitoring scripts
- Multi-language citizen versions
- Accessibility-first document views

## What This Repo Now Adds

This roadmap is paired with:
- `docs/digital-issues/`: citizen-facing playbook stubs for common online problems
- `data/national-authorities/`: schema and starter routing layer for national digital enforcement bodies

## Operating Rule

When choosing what to add next, prefer things that reduce one of these barriers:
- uncertainty about where to start
- evidence loss
- deadline loss
- cross-border confusion
- legal-language overload
- dependence on insider knowledge
