# AI Decision Harm Or Opaque Automated Output

## Typical Problem

An AI-supported system made or shaped a decision about moderation, ranking, access, pricing, fraud, eligibility, safety, or employment screening, and you cannot tell how to challenge it.

## Save First

- the decision notice or output
- screenshots of the result
- all prompts or inputs you gave if relevant
- the date and context
- any model or tool name shown
- policies, terms, and help pages
- any evidence of harm

## First Route

- ask the provider or deployer what system was used
- ask what review process exists
- ask whether a human can re-check the outcome

## Likely Public Routes

- data protection authority if personal data or profiling is involved
- consumer authority if the AI system is part of a commercial interface or unfair practice
- sector regulator if the system operates in a regulated field

## EU Layer

Depending on the issue, the AI Act, GDPR, consumer law, and product or sector rules may all matter. The citizen problem is often procedural opacity first, legal categorisation second.

## Escalate When

- the provider refuses to identify the review route
- the decision had concrete impact
- the system looks systematically biased or unsafe

## Useful Ask

Ask for:
- whether the decision was automated, assisted, or human-made
- review and correction route
- record of the grounds used
- contact point for complaints
