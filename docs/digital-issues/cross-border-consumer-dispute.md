# Cross-Border Online Consumer Dispute

## Typical Problem

You bought a digital or physical service across borders in the EU and the trader, platform, or intermediary is using distance, language, or jurisdictional confusion to avoid resolution.

## Save First

- contract or order confirmation
- trader identity and country shown
- payment proof
- shipping or access logs
- support correspondence
- cancellation or refund policy

## First Route

- complain directly to the trader
- use the platform resolution route if one exists
- preserve the trader identity information exactly as shown at purchase

## Likely Public Routes

- national consumer authority
- European Consumer Centre network where relevant
- sector regulator if the service is communications, travel, finance, or another regulated field

## EU Layer

Cross-border disputes are often easier to understand when split into:
- contract or payment issue
- trader identity issue
- platform governance issue
- regulator or ADR support issue

## Escalate When

- the trader relies on jurisdiction confusion
- the platform refuses to identify the responsible seller
- payment and refund channels both stall

## Useful Ask

Ask for:
- trader legal identity
- refund basis and deadline
- governing terms shown at purchase
- internal decision record if the platform rejected your claim
