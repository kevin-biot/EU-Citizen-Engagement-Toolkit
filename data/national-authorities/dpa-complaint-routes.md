# DPA Complaint Routes

Source: [dpa-complaint-routes.csv](/Users/kevinbrown/EU-Citizen-Engagement-Toolkit/data/national-authorities/dpa-complaint-routes.csv:1)

This is a first campaign-ready pass for GDPR complaint intake routes. It goes beyond naming the DPA by mapping the actual official complaint-entry surface, the expected channel, and the most important filing caveats.

Current coverage in this pass: `27` Member States (`France`, `Germany`, `Ireland`, `Italy`, `Netherlands`, `Spain`, `Poland`, `Austria`, `Belgium`, `Denmark`, `Sweden`, `Finland`, `Luxembourg`, `Portugal`, `Czechia`, `Slovakia`, `Croatia`, `Estonia`, `Latvia`, `Lithuania`, `Romania`, `Bulgaria`, `Cyprus`, `Greece`, `Hungary`, `Malta`, `Slovenia`).

Use this file when a GDPR issue has already moved past a basic rights request and you need to know:

- where the complaint actually starts
- whether the authority expects you to contact the controller first
- which channel is official
- whether there is a useful timing or follow-up signal on the official page
- whether jurisdiction or scope is a major issue

| Italy | [Garante](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/) | [Modulistica](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/garante/doc.jsp?ID=2014184) | Official forms and services pages | Use the official complaint model or service route published by the Garante; rights-request disputes should usually be documented before escalation. | No short timing statement was captured in this first pass; verify current handling notes on the forms page before filing. | Central Italian DPA route; useful for rights, complaint, and formal filing workflows. |
| Netherlands | [Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens](https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/) | [Submitting a tip-off or a complaint](https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/submitting-a-tip-off-or-a-complaint-to-the-dutch-dpa) | Online complaint or tip-off form | The AP says you must first complain to the organisation itself or make a rights request before filing with the AP. | The AP says you will hear from it within `three months`; it may investigate directly or forward a cross-border case while staying your contact point. | Useful for Netherlands-based complaints and EU-headquartered-in-the-Netherlands cross-border cases. |
| Spain | [AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/) | [How to file a complaint](https://www.aepd.es/preguntas-frecuentes/13-reclamaciones/FAQ-1301-como-puedo-interponer-una-reclamacion-si-han-vulnerado-mis-datos-de-caracter-personal) | Electronic Office complaint forms | AEPD expects evidence or indications of infringement and uses different filing paths depending on the complaint type. | AEPD says it has up to `three months` to decide on admission or non-admission; after that the complaint is deemed admitted if no decision has issued. | Strong route for Spain-based rights and complaint filings, especially where the issue fits a standard AEPD category. |
| Poland | [UODO](https://uodo.gov.pl/) | [If you want to lodge a complaint](https://uodo.gov.pl/en/681/1404) | Traditional written complaint, electronic inbox, ePUAP, or `biznes.gov.pl` | Use the official complaint route and channel guidance; electronic filing is channel-specific and form-based. | UODO says that after investigating the case, if an infringement is confirmed, the President can order compliance by administrative decision. | Useful first route for Poland-based GDPR complaints; the contact page explains the practical differences between filing channels. |
| Austria | [DSB](https://dsb.gv.at/) | [Beschwerde](https://dsb.gv.at/eingabe-an-die-dsb/beschwerde) | Online complaint form, email, or post | This is a formal legal remedy, so identify the opposing party, the allegedly violated right, and the timing clearly before filing. | No short processing-time target was captured in this pass, but the page explains filing deadlines: normally within `one year` from knowledge and no later than `three years` after the event. | Useful for Austria-based complaints; cross-border private-sector cases can trigger one-stop-shop cooperation and take longer. |
| Belgium | [APD-GBA](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/) | [Introduire une plainte](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/citoyen/agir/introduire-une-plainte) | Complaint page during portal transition; verify the live citizen portal before filing | The authority strongly suggests checking whether mediation or an information request is more appropriate first. Rights-based complaints normally require prior exercise of rights and waiting `one month` for the controller's response. | As of `April 24, 2026`, the authority says electronic citizen forms are temporarily unavailable and a new citizen portal is due to open on `May 8, 2026`. | Important route for Belgium-based complaints, but the current portal transition matters. |
| Denmark | [Datatilsynet](https://www.datatilsynet.dk/) | [Sådan klager du](https://www.datatilsynet.dk/borger/klage/saadan-klager-du) | MitID-backed complaint form, with fallback contact routes if the form cannot be used | Datatilsynet recommends contacting the controller first. It requires at minimum a name and address and does not normally handle anonymous complaints. | The authority states a service goal that `50%` of complaint cases should finish within `two months` and `80%` within `six months`. | Good first route for Denmark-based complaints, especially where the complainant can use the official form with MitID. |
| Sweden | [IMY](https://www.imy.se/) | [File a GDPR complaint](https://www.imy.se/en/individuals/forms-and-e-services/file-a-gdpr-complaint/) | IMY e-service, with email or letter fallback in some cases | IMY recommends contacting the organisation first, especially for access, erasure, or rectification disputes. | IMY says it always replies if the complaint meets the formal requirements and sends a copy of the inspection decision if an inspection is opened. | Strong route for Sweden-based complaints; the page also notes that the complainant may choose another EEA authority for cross-border cases. |
| Finland | [Data Protection Ombudsman](https://tietosuoja.fi/en/home) | [Processing of matters within our competence](https://tietosuoja.fi/en/processing-of-matters-within-our-competence) | Email or post to the Office; verify the current contact path before filing | No direct complaint-form page was located in this pass, so this route is based on the authority's legal-protection and case-processing pages. | The authority says instituted cases receive a record number and that decisions can be appealed to the competent Administrative Court. | Official route, but weaker than the other rows because the practical complaint-entry page still needs a cleaner source pass. |
| Luxembourg | [CNPD](https://cnpd.public.lu/en.html) | [Complaint form](https://cnpd.public.lu/en/particuliers/faire-valoir/formulaire-plainte.html) | Official complaint form | The CNPD asks data subjects to contact the controller first to assert their rights before filing, unless that is difficult or impossible. | The CNPD says it first checks whether the complaint is justified and whether the alleged facts are likely to amount to an infringement. | Strong route for Luxembourg-based complaints once a rights request has failed or is impracticable. |
| Portugal | [CNPD](https://www.cnpd.pt/) | [Participações](https://www.cnpd.pt/cidadaos/participacoes/) | Written online complaint forms by issue type | The complainant needs to choose the form that matches the subject matter; if the issue is really an information request, a different path should be used. | The authority says the forms are designed to gather the minimum information needed for a preliminary analysis and faster routing. | Good Portugal route because the CNPD now exposes a citizen-facing complaints section with a general form and topic-specific paths. |
| Czechia | [UOOU](https://uoou.gov.cz/) | [Complaint against a controller or processor](https://uoou.gov.cz/verejnost/stiznost-na-spravce-nebo-zpracovatele) | Official complaint page and issue-specific status tools | Use the official page to confirm the issue is really within UOOU competence; the page explicitly excludes several non-data-protection matters. | The authority also exposes a status-check tool for at least some complaint categories. | Useful route for Czechia-based complaints once the issue is confirmed as a true personal-data processing matter. |
| Slovakia | [Office for Personal Data Protection](https://www.dataprotection.gov.sk/en/) | [Proceedings on protection of personal data](https://www.dataprotection.gov.sk/en/rights-data-subjects/proceedings-on-protection-personal-data/) | Written filing, authorised electronic filing, or personal oral submission into minutes | The complaint must be submitted in Slovak. | The authority says an ordinary unauthorised email must be completed within `three working days` in writing, with authorised electronic filing, or personally into minutes. | Strong route for Slovakia-based complaints; the channel rules matter because ordinary email alone is not enough. |
| Croatia | [AZOP](https://azop.hr/) | [Data subject rights](https://azop.hr/data-subject-rights/) | Personal filing, post, fax, or email | Anyone who believes a GDPR right has been violated may submit a request for determination of a rights violation. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass. | Useful Croatia route for rights-based complaints and straightforward supervisory requests. |
| Estonia | [AKI](https://www.aki.ee/en) | [Complaint in a personal-data matter](https://www.aki.ee/meist/vota-uhendust/kaebus-isikuandmete-kaitse-asjas) | Complaint link plus digitally signed electronic submission or post | The inspectorate routes the filing according to its legal nature, so the same submission may be treated as a complaint, challenge, or memorandum depending on content. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass. | Useful official route for Estonia, though the live complaint page is currently exposed through the Estonian-language contact path. |
| Latvia | [DVI](https://www.dvi.gov.lv/en) | [Complaint concerning the processing of personal data](https://www.dvi.gov.lv/en/services/complaint-concerning-processing-personal-data) | Complaint service page with official complaint sample | The complaint should identify the processor, the relationship to that processor, the nature of the processing, and whether the complainant already contacted the processor. | The service page says the Inspectorate shall provide a response no later than `three months`. | Strong route for Latvia-based complaints with a clear official service page and timing signal. |
| Lithuania | [VDAI](https://vdai.lrv.lt/en/) | [Recommended complaint form](https://vdai.lrv.lt/uploads/vdai/documents/files/Recommended%20form%20of%20the%20complaint%20%202018%20EN.pdf) | Recommended complaint form plus written submission rules | The complaint form expects identification of the respondent, the relationship, the subject matter, whether the controller was contacted first, and attached evidence. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass. | Useful Lithuania route because the official site exposes the complaint form and evidentiary structure clearly, though the practical filing route is more formal than some peers. |
| Romania | [ANSPDCP](https://www.dataprotection.ro/) | [Complaints page](https://www.dataprotection.ro/?page=Plangeri_pagina_principala) | Registry, post, email, or electronic form | Romania expects the complainant first to address the controller for GDPR rights and then complain with evidence if no answer arrives within `one month` or the answer is unsatisfactory. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass, but the procedure page sets out detailed admissibility and evidence rules. | Strong route for Romania-based complaints once the rights-request stage and evidentiary threshold are met. |
| Bulgaria | [CPDP](https://cpdp.bg/en/) | [Lodging complaints and alerts](https://cpdp.bg/en/lodging-complaints-and-alerts/) | In person, by post, fax, email with qualified electronic signature, or via the secure electronic service system | Complaints and alerts must be translated into Bulgarian. | The CPDP page sets out required contents and says email complaints must be electronic documents signed with a qualified electronic signature. | Strong Bulgaria route because the official page is explicit about channels and formal requirements. |
| Cyprus | [Cypriot DPA](https://www.dataprotection.gov.cy/) | [Investigation procedure PDF](https://www.dataprotection.gov.cy/dataprotection/dataprotection.nsf/59EA353334A12A41C225826000291BFC/$file/Investigation%20procedure.pdf) | Form B complaint route and investigation procedure guidance | The authority uses a specific complaint form for breaches of the legislation for the processing of personal data. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass. | Useful Cyprus route, but this row should be refined later with a cleaner web page if one becomes available. |
| Greece | [HDPA](https://www.dpa.gr/en) | [Complaint to the Hellenic DPA](https://www.dpa.gr/en/individuals/complaint-to-the-hellenic-dpa) | Online portal with Greek login, or English-by-email fallback in some cross-border cases | For rights complaints, the complainant should normally have exercised rights with the controller first and then waited `one month` or received an unsatisfactory answer. | The page explains that complaints should use the appropriate form with supporting documents and notes an English-by-email fallback in some cases. | Strong Greece route for domestic and cross-border matters where Greece is the place of the alleged infringement. |
| Hungary | [NAIH](https://www.naih.hu/) | [Investigation by the Authority](https://www.naih.hu/data-protection/investigation-by-the-authority) | Authority inquiry or administrative procedure for data protection | NAIH distinguishes between inquiries and formal administrative procedures, so the complainant route depends on the kind of intervention sought. | No short timing statement was captured in this pass. | Useful Hungary route, though it still needs a later pass to locate the clearest citizen-facing filing instructions or forms. |
| Malta | [IDPC](https://idpc.org.mt/) | [Contact / file a complaint](https://idpc.org.mt/contact/) | Dedicated online complaint form | Malta expects engagement with the controller first in rights-based cases and then a complaint if there is no answer within `one month` or the answer is unsatisfactory. | The contact page says electronic data protection complaints shall only be lodged using the dedicated online form. | Strong Malta route because the authority clearly separates the complaint form from general contact. |
| Slovenia | [IP-RS](https://www.ip-rs.si/en/) | [How to file an application](https://www.ip-rs.si/en/data-protection/how-to-file-an-application) | Online form, email, or ordinary mail | Anyone can submit a report if they feel someone is violating the GDPR or the Slovenian Personal Data Protection Act. | The authority says it will try to provide an answer in the shortest time possible. | Strong Slovenia route for reports and complaint-style supervisory interventions. |

## Notes

- This file is intentionally campaign-oriented, not exhaustive. It focuses on the official entry point and practical filing constraints rather than reproducing the full legal basis.
- The German row is especially important to read carefully because `BfDI` is not the right authority for most normal private-company complaints.
- The Dutch and Irish routes both explicitly expect prior contact with the organisation before the authority filing.
- The Spanish route is highly category-driven, so choosing the right complaint path matters.
- The Polish route gives multiple filing channels, but the official page distinguishes between the inbox-based electronic route and fallback email use.
- Belgium is in a live filing-channel transition in this snapshot, so the complaint page itself must be checked before action.
- Denmark and Sweden both strongly prefer that the complainant first contact the organisation.
- Austria treats the complaint route as a formal legal remedy and sets out filing deadlines clearly.
- Finland still needs a cleaner complaint-entry source in a later pass, even though the legal-protection route is now mapped.
- Portugal is currently usable because the CNPD exposes issue-specific complaint forms rather than only a generic homepage route.
- Slovakia's filing-channel rule matters: plain email on its own is not sufficient unless completed quickly through an accepted channel.
- Estonia currently exposes the complaint route through a contact-path page rather than a clean English complaint form.
- Lithuania's English materials are unusually form-heavy and include a recommended complaint form PDF that is useful even before a more polished route is found.
- Romania is strong on admissibility detail and multilingual filing, but the complainant still needs to pass the prior-controller and evidence threshold.
- Bulgaria is formal about language and signature requirements, especially for email complaints.
- Cyprus is now covered but still deserves a better web-native route than the current PDF-based procedure note.
- Hungary is the weakest of the final rows because the authority explains the procedure types more clearly than the citizen entry point.
- Malta is unusually clear that electronic complaints should go only through the dedicated online form.
