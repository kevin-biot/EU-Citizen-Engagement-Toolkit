# National Digital Authorities

This section is for country-by-country authority mapping that citizens can use when a digital problem needs a national enforcement route as well as, or instead of, an EU institutional route.

## Why This Exists

Most real digital harms are not solved by Brussels alone.

Citizens often need to identify:
- the national Data Protection Authority
- the national Digital Services Coordinator
- the consumer protection authority
- the telecom, media, or communications regulator
- the competition authority
- the accessibility or equality body

## Files

- `national-digital-authorities.csv`: first-pass per-country digital enforcement directory
- `national-digital-authorities.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the same authority directory
- `dpa-complaint-routes.csv`: first campaign-ready map of official GDPR complaint entry routes for high-use Member States
- `dpa-complaint-routes.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the DPA complaint-entry layer
- `gdpr-cross-border-reference.csv`: official-source reference index for one-stop-shop, lead-authority, and cross-border GDPR complaint handling
- `gdpr-cross-border-reference.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the cross-border reference layer
- `national-cpc-authorities.csv`: detailed CPC consumer-protection entries from the official Commission source
- `national-cpc-authorities.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the CPC layer
- `national-equality-bodies.csv`: official Commission-listed national equality bodies
- `national-equality-bodies.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the equality-body layer
- `national-web-accessibility-bodies.csv`: official web-accessibility monitoring, reporting, and enforcement bodies by country and section
- `national-web-accessibility-bodies.md`: human-readable Markdown view of the accessibility body layer
- `issue-router.csv`: starter map from common citizen problem types to likely first routes

## Intended Use

Use this data to decide:
- where to complain first
- which route is national
- whether an issue needs multiple filings
- which authority is procedural versus substantive
- when a summary routing file is enough and when a detailed sector-specific list is required

## Limits

- Enforcement design differs by Member State.
- Some roles are split across more than one body.
- Some routes are national, regional, or sectoral rather than single-agency.
- The CPC source is detailed and heterogeneous: some rows are broad national bodies, while others are sectoral or regional.
- Accessibility enforcement is often split across monitoring, reporting, and enforcement bodies, and in some countries also across subnational authorities.
- This section is a routing aid, not a substitute for checking the latest official authority page.
